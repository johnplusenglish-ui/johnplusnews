A famous footballer has left the brand that supported him for almost twenty years to help a newcomer build its first football boots.

Core version

Imagine you have been in the same group for twenty years. Everyone knows it, and it is very successful. Then a small, new group asks you to help build something. Would you stay, or would you change?

On 18 September 2026, the footballer Kylian Mbappé made a choice like this. Since 2006, the sports company Nike had made his boots and clothes. Now he has left Nike and joined On, a Swiss sportswear company that wants to grow beyond running and tennis into football.

This is a sponsorship deal. It is not a move to a new football team. A sponsor pays a player, and the player wears and promotes its products. Mbappé will be a global ambassador for On. He will also work with the company's product teams to develop and test future boots and clothes.

The money is a secret. On said the deal includes cash and equity, which means a share, or stake, in the company, but it did not say how much. Nike said it was proud of what they achieved together. Mbappé wrote online that this is a big change in his life. He said he is now working with people who want to try new ideas.

On says its first football boots are planned for 2027. It also named the former France star Thierry Henry as its director of football. The company says top players will test its products from the start. Remember, these are the company's own plans. We do not know yet if the boots will be better than other boots.

So, would you rather join a successful team or help a new team get started? A famous place can give you experience and support. A new project can give you more freedom to try your own ideas. Neither choice is always right. It depends on what you want to learn.

Intermediate version

Imagine you have belonged to the same group for twenty years. It is famous, it is successful, and people know you as part of it. Then a smaller group arrives with an unusual offer: come and help us build something new. Would the safe choice still be the best one?

In September 2026, Kylian Mbappé faced a choice like that, off the pitch. On 18 September, the Swiss company On announced that it was entering football, with Mbappé as a global ambassador. Reuters reported that his partnership with Nike began in 2006, when he was nine years old.

It helps to be clear about what this deal is. It is a sponsorship deal, not a transfer, so Mbappé has not joined a new football team. Instead, he has changed the company that supplies and promotes his boots and clothing. On says he will also work directly with its product teams on the development and testing of future football footwear and clothes.

The terms were not made public. According to Reuters, On said the deal includes cash and equity, which means a share in the company itself, but it gave no figure. Nike said it was proud of what it had achieved with the player. Mbappé wrote on social media that the move is a big change in his life and that he is now working with people who share his dreams.

On's co-founder and co-CEO, David Allemann, said football needs new ideas rather than more of the same. The company also named the former France star Thierry Henry as its director of football. It said that a Swiss international player, Sydney Schertenleib, will help to test products, including ones for the women's game.

The plans are ambitious, but they are still plans. On says its development will be athlete-led, with elite players testing and improving products. Reuters reports that the first boots are planned for 2027. Until they exist, claims that they will be lighter or more precise than other boots are promotion, not proof. Wearing a brand is not the same as a product being tested and proven, so it is fair to wait and see.

For a teenager, the most interesting part is the choice itself. A famous team gives you experience, stability and people who already know what works. A new project offers less certainty, but your ideas might matter more. Imagine choosing a partner for a school project. Would you pick the most experienced person, the most enthusiastic one, or the one who really listens?

Advanced version

Kylian Mbappé has a new sportswear partner, and some of what it promises has not been made yet. On 18 September 2026, the Swiss company On announced him as a global ambassador, ending his long association with Nike. This was a sponsorship deal, not a move to a new football club. The agreement also gave him a role in developing products that had yet to reach the pitch.

Reuters reported that Mbappé's relationship with Nike began in 2006, when he was nine. He has not joined a new club. He has changed the company that makes and promotes his equipment, and according to On he will also work directly with its product teams on developing and testing future footwear and clothing.

The most interesting detail is what has not been said. On said the agreement includes cash and equity, but no amount was disclosed. Equity, in general, means owning a small share of a company, so an owner benefits if the company grows. That would make this more than a fee for appearing in advertisements, because the athlete has a stake in whether the project succeeds. How large that stake is remains unknown.

Both sides offered reasons, and each deserves to be read as what it is: a statement from someone with an interest. Mbappé wrote that football had taken him through a big change and that he was now surrounded by innovators who share his dreams. David Allemann, On's co-CEO, said football needs new ideas rather than more of the same. Nike said it was proud of what it had achieved with him on and off the pitch.

Several of the claims in On's announcement are promotional. The company says its development will be athlete-led, with elite players involved from the start, and it describes its future products in glowing terms. It has named the former France star Thierry Henry as director of football and said that the Swiss international Sydney Schertenleib will help test products, including some for the women's game. Reuters reports that the first boots are planned for 2027. Until players wear them in matches, nobody can say whether they are better than anything else on the market.

What makes the story relevant beyond football is the choice underneath it. Staying with a famous partner offers experience, stability and a proven way of working. Joining a newcomer offers less certainty, but a bigger voice in what gets built. Neither is automatically the braver or the wiser option. Someone who walks away from a successful group might be chasing influence, or might simply be bored. Someone who stays might be loyal, or might simply be comfortable.

Perhaps the best test is a practical one. Think about a group you belong to, or a project you have joined. If somebody offered you more say but fewer guarantees, which would you want to know first: what you could lose, or what you could shape?
