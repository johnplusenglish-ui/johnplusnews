Core version

Maybe you have seen the video. A boy stands at the very front of a long boat. He wears sunglasses and moves his arms slowly and calmly. People all over the world copied his dance. But do you know where it comes from?

The boy is Rayyan Arkan Dikha, from Indonesia. In 2025, he was eleven years old. He told the BBC that he made up the dance himself. He said it was spontaneous, which means he did not plan it.

The video was filmed at a boat race called Pacu Jalur. "Pacu" means race, and "jalur" means the long boats. In the video, at least 11 adults are rowing. At the front of the boat there is a dancer, called the Togak Luan. His job is to give the crew energy. Rayyan was the dancer. It was his first time at the national race.

On the internet, people called his style "aura farming". This phrase means looking cool and building your "aura", which is a word for charisma. Soon, famous sports teams and players, such as the football club Paris Saint-Germain, copied the moves. Millions of people watched the videos.

Rayyan's mother was proud, but she was also worried that he might fall. She said there is a rescue team near the boats. The government of Indonesia noticed him too. The governor of his province named him a cultural ambassador.

This story shows something about the internet. A short video can travel far, but it can lose its story. Rayyan was not only "cool". He was doing a real job in a real tradition. When you share something online, what should stay with it? Where it came from, and who made it, are good things to remember.

Intermediate version

You have probably seen the clip, even if you did not know what it was. A boy in sunglasses stands on the tip of a long, fast boat. He blows kisses to each side and moves his arms in slow, smooth patterns, almost without expression. By July 2025, people across the world were copying him. So what were they actually copying?

The boy is Rayyan Arkan Dikha, who was eleven at the time and comes from a village in Kuantan Singingi Regency, in Indonesia. He told BBC Indonesia that he created the moves himself and that the dance was spontaneous.

The clip was filmed at Pacu Jalur, a traditional boat race. "Pacu" means race and "jalur" refers to the long, canoe-like boats. In the video, the boat is rowed by at least eleven adults, and the dancer at the front has a particular role. He is called the Togak Luan, and his job is to energise the crew. Rayyan was making his debut at the national race, wearing a traditional outfit and a headcloth.

Online, the clip became a symbol of "aura farming", an internet phrase for looking cool and building your "aura", a word for charisma. The BBC reported that videos with this tag had millions of views on TikTok by early July. The football club Paris Saint-Germain and the American football player Travis Kelce posted their own versions.

The attention reached Indonesia's government too. The culture minister, Fadli Zon, said that dancing at the front of the boat is not easy. The dancer must keep his balance while also motivating the rowers. Rayyan was named a cultural ambassador by the governor of his province. His mother, Rani Ridawati, said she worries that he might fall, but that he is a strong swimmer and a rescue team is nearby.

There is a difference between the dance, the race and the label. The dance belongs to Rayyan. The race is a tradition with its own rules and its own meaning for the people who take part. "Aura farming" is a phrase that the internet attached afterwards. The viral clip joined all three together, and most viewers saw only the cool part.

That is why the story is more interesting than a meme. When something travels far, a lot of its original meaning can be left behind. Advanced version

At the front of a racing boat in Indonesia, eleven-year-old Rayyan Arkan Dikha danced while the rowers worked behind him. The clip travelled far beyond the race. Viewers called it aura farming: a performance of effortless cool. Learning his name and the dancer's job changes what there is to see.

The boy is Rayyan Arkan Dikha, then eleven, from Kuantan Singingi Regency in Indonesia. The BBC reported that he told BBC Indonesia he invented the moves himself and that it was spontaneous. The clip comes from the national Pacu Jalur boat race, where "pacu" means race and "jalur" refers to the long, canoe-like boats. Rayyan was making his debut as the Togak Luan, the dancer at the tip of the boat, whose role is to energise the crew.

In the video, at least eleven adults are rowing, and he stands at the front in a traditional outfit with a headcloth, blowing kisses to either side before moving his arms with a calm, expressionless confidence. Online, that calm became the point. The clip came to stand for "aura farming", an internet phrase for the act of looking cool and building your "aura", or charisma. Sports teams and players, including Paris Saint-Germain and the American football player Travis Kelce, posted their own versions.

Three things are tangled together here, and it helps to separate them. The dance was Rayyan's own moment of improvisation. The race is a tradition with its own rules, in which the dancer has a job to do for his crew. And "aura farming" is a label that internet users attached later.

The difference matters because meaning changes in transit. For the rowers, the dancer is part of the effort. For millions of viewers, he is a performer displaying effortless cool. Neither reading is foolish, but only one of them knows what the dance is for. Indonesia's culture minister, Fadli Zon, pointed to the practical side: dancing at the front of the boat is not simple, because the dancer must keep his balance while motivating the crew, which he suggested is why children are often chosen.

Rayyan's own response was modest. He said he was happy that people around the world had noticed, although he did not recognise the famous people who had copied him. He was named a cultural ambassador by the governor of his province, and his mother said her main worry was that he might fall, although he is a strong swimmer and a rescue team is in place. His dream, he told the BBC, is to become a police officer.

So what should travel with a clip? A name would be a start, and so would a few words about the place and the purpose. Copying a dance can be a compliment, but credit is what turns a copy into a conversation. When a local tradition becomes internationally famous, outsiders can choose to learn something from it, or to enjoy the surface and move on. Most of us do a little of both.
