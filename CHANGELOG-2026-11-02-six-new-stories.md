Sources
-------

Story 1 came with its source and it was re-read before writing. Story 2 came with a
source. The other four were identified here and read in full:

1. The Guardian, 27 October 2026, Sardinian restaurants ban families from using phones
   at the table.
2. The Guardian, 1 November 2026, Why you should treat charisma as a red flag.
3. The Guardian, 30 October 2026, 'Ceilidh renaissance' lures gen Z offline in search of
   real-life joy.
4. The Guardian, 26 October 2026, 'Maxxing' is Dictionary.com's word of the year, used
   for the factual spine, with the Polly Hudson column of 1 November 2026 used for the
   joke it proposes. The column is the source the draft pointed at; on its own it carries
   almost no verifiable fact, so the word-of-the-year piece supplies the reporting and
   the column is clearly labelled as comedy in all three levels.
5. The Guardian, 1 November 2026, The kindness of strangers: In 1989 I lost a wallet with
   $1,000 in it.
6. The Guardian, 31 October 2026, Don't make sugar a treat, or hide the broccoli: top
   chefs on how to make your kids happy eaters.

Where this departs from the draft
---------------------------------

1. **Questions are per level, not shared.** The draft says the questions are shared across
   levels "as in the existing editorial guide". Every one of the 111 existing stories uses
   per-level sets, so all six here have their own Before you read and Talk about it for
   Core, Intermediate and Advanced. The draft's shared set for story 1 is kept as its Core
   set.
2. **No em dashes.** The draft's prose uses them; no story in the catalogue does, so they
   were rewritten out.
3. **Story 1 word counts.** The draft's Core and Intermediate ran short of the house
   median and its Advanced ran long. Two short paragraphs of sourced material were added
   and the Advanced closing was tightened.

Editorial decisions
-------------------

- Story 2 is an opinion column, and it is attributed as one throughout. Confidence is
  never presented as evidence of dishonesty: the Core level says plainly that a confident
  person is not necessarily lying, and the Advanced level argues against the original
  headline. The piece's political example and its reference to a named child-abuse case
  were left out.
- Story 3's Eventbrite growth figures are attributed to one ticketing platform, and the
  Advanced level says explicitly that they describe a market rather than a culture.
- Story 4 says at every level that minning is one columnist's joke and not a measured
  trend. The suffix's origins are described as online forums about appearance, without
  repeating the detail already covered by the looksmaxxing story.
- Story 5 is one person's recollection of 1989, published in 2026, and the Advanced level
  states that a single anecdote is not evidence about human nature.
- Story 6 is nine individual households, not dietary advice, and the Advanced level says
  so directly.
