title: Where Might You Travel in 2027?
summary: Expedia says searches are growing for Busan, Perugia, Punta del Este and Holbox. But a search is not a booking.

Where will people want to go on holiday in 2027? The travel company Expedia has made a prediction. Paris and Rome will still attract visitors, but its report says searches are growing for places that receive less attention.

In October 2026, it published a list of destinations with fast-growing searches for flights and places to stay. The list included Busan in South Korea, Perugia in Italy, Punta del Este in Uruguay and Holbox in Mexico. These are very different places, from busy cities to relaxed coastal destinations.

Busan is known for beaches, markets and city life. Perugia offers historic streets, food and cultural festivals. Punta del Este attracts visitors to its Atlantic beaches, while Holbox is an island where tourists come for nature and a slower pace. Each place offers something different from the usual holiday in a famous capital.

However, searches do not prove that millions of tourists will arrive next year. Expedia compared interest on its own platforms with the previous period. People might search for a destination and then choose somewhere else. Other travel companies may also see different patterns.

A place becoming popular can be good for local businesses, but it can also bring problems. More visitors may mean higher prices, crowded streets and pressure on the environment. A destination that looks peaceful in a photograph may feel very different during its busiest month.

Before choosing a trending destination, it is sensible to do your homework. Look at the weather for the month you want to travel and find out whether local buses and trains are convenient. Some places become very busy during school holidays, festivals or special events. A beautiful island may be hard to get to, and a famous restaurant may be fully booked.

You can also think about how visitors affect people who live there all year. Respecting local rules, avoiding unnecessary waste and supporting local services can make travel better for everyone. Online lists are useful for picking up ideas, but they cannot tell you what will suit your family, health or budget. A less famous place may offer an amazing holiday, but it still needs careful planning.

Perhaps the best travel choice is not the place everybody is talking about. It is the place that matches your interests, budget and the way you enjoy spending time. A travel trend can help you discover somewhere new, but it should not make the decision for you.

=== Intermediate ===

title: Can Travel Searches Predict Your Next Holiday?
summary: Expedia compared searches on its own platforms from September 2025 to July 2026. Rising curiosity is not the same as arrivals, and some of these places are busy already.

Every year brings a new list of destinations that travellers are supposedly about to discover. Some are already famous, while others have spent years outside the international spotlight. What makes a location suddenly attractive, and can a travel company really predict where people will go next?

A city may suddenly pop up everywhere online after a popular film, a new flight route or a few beautiful photographs. The buzz can build long before most travellers know much about it.

Expedia's Unpack '27 report, released in October 2026, identified destinations where searches for flights and accommodation had grown compared with the previous period. Its selections included Busan in South Korea, the Italian city of Perugia, Holbox in Mexico and Uruguay's Punta del Este. The list also featured places in Japan, Australia, Canada, Britain and the United States.

The destinations appeal to different kinds of travellers. Busan combines beaches with lively urban markets, while Perugia offers medieval streets and cultural festivals. Holbox is known for its quieter island atmosphere, and Punta del Este attracts holidaymakers to Atlantic beaches and seaside restaurants. Rather than following one theme, the list reflects growing curiosity about a wide variety of places.

The numbers deserve careful reading. Expedia compared search activity on its own platforms between September 2025 and July 2026 with a similar earlier period. Rising interest does not necessarily translate into completed bookings or future arrivals, and the results may differ from what other companies are seeing. There is no perfect destination for everybody. Someone looking for quiet walks may dislike the nightlife another visitor loves, so following a trend can leave people disappointed.

Increased attention also creates challenges. Tourism supports jobs and businesses, but sudden popularity can push up accommodation costs and place pressure on local infrastructure. Travellers may be attracted by images of quiet streets only to discover that other visitors have had the same idea.

The list also raises the question of what travellers should do when a destination receives sudden attention. Before booking, it is worth looking into transport options, seasonal crowding, prices and whether visitors can access local attractions without placing unnecessary pressure on residents. Some destinations highlighted by Expedia may already have significant tourism, so the description 'hidden gem' can be misleading.

Local authorities may welcome extra visitors while worrying about housing costs and environmental damage. A company's ranking is only one piece of information, and its commercial interests are not the same as those of a community deciding how much tourism it wants.

A useful travel list should therefore start a conversation rather than settle it. Where do you actually want to spend time, and what kind of experience are you hoping for? Choosing a destination because it fits your interests may be more rewarding than following the latest prediction about where everybody else is heading.

=== Advanced ===

title: When a Travel Report Puts a Quiet Place on the Map
summary: Expedia's 2027 report says searches are rising for Busan, Perugia, Punta del Este and Holbox. A search is not a booking, and a quiet place may not stay quiet.

A city that barely appeared in last year's holiday plans can suddenly become next year's must-visit destination. A new travel report puts places including Busan, Perugia and Punta del Este in the spotlight. Its predictions may offer ideas for a trip, but interest in a place is not the same as bookings, and neither guarantees a good holiday.

Predictions about the next fashionable destination have a way of becoming self-fulfilling. Media coverage generates curiosity, curiosity increases searches, and the resulting figures produce yet more headlines.

Expedia's October 2026 Unpack '27 report used year-on-year growth in flight and accommodation searches to identify destinations attracting increasing interest. Among those highlighted were Busan in South Korea, Perugia in Italy, Holbox in Mexico and Punta del Este in Uruguay. The wider list also included Hyogo, the Tweed region, Chichester and several North American destinations.

The geographical range is part of the appeal. Busan combines metropolitan energy with beaches, Perugia offers a historic hilltop setting, and Punta del Este has long been associated with coastal holidays. Holbox promises a different pace on an island where vehicle access is limited. Such places are not necessarily undiscovered; many already have substantial tourism economies and established identities. That does not make the data worthless. It simply means that search growth is a measure of changing interest, not an impartial ranking of the world's most rewarding places.

The report's methodology is essential to understanding its claims. Expedia compared searches on its platforms from September 2025 to July 2026 against the corresponding earlier period. A percentage increase measures changes in interest within that system, not global visitor numbers or a guarantee of future popularity. A smaller destination may show a dramatic growth rate without attracting more tourists than a major capital.

There is also a paradox in promoting supposedly quieter alternatives. Publicity can redirect visitor pressure rather than reduce it. Rising demand may benefit restaurants and hotels but can also strain housing, transport and natural environments. Any discussion of emerging destinations should consider residents' priorities and the capacity of local infrastructure. Readers can use these lists as a starting point while checking seasonal conditions, local concerns and what they actually enjoy. Travel decisions deserve more than a fashionable postcode.

The fact that a company has recorded growing search interest does not establish that a place would benefit from substantial additional tourism. Travellers could therefore weigh up seasonal patterns, public transport, local accommodation policies and alternative periods for visiting before making plans. The most useful forecast encourages informed curiosity rather than a race to arrive before everybody else. Popularity alone is a poor substitute for understanding a place.

Before booking a place from a trending list, it helps to ask why it appeals, what kind of experience it offers and whether extra attention will change it. The best destination for 2027 will not be the same for everybody, whatever a ranking says.
