=== Core ===

Where will people want to go on holiday in 2027? Paris and Rome will still attract visitors, but a new travel report suggests that many people are looking at places that receive less attention. In October 2026, Expedia published a list of destinations with fast-growing searches for flights and places to stay. The list included Busan in South Korea, Perugia in Italy, Punta del Este in Uruguay and Holbox in Mexico. These are very different places, from busy cities to relaxed coastal destinations.

Busan is known for beaches, markets and city life. Perugia offers historic streets, food and cultural festivals. Punta del Este attracts visitors to its Atlantic beaches, while Holbox is an island where tourists come for nature and a slower pace. Each place offers something different from the usual holiday in a famous capital.

However, searches do not prove that millions of tourists will arrive next year. Expedia compared interest on its own platforms with the previous period. People might search for a destination and then choose somewhere else. Other travel companies may also see different patterns.

A place becoming popular can be good for local businesses, but it can also bring problems. More visitors may mean higher prices, crowded streets and pressure on the environment. A destination that looks peaceful in a photograph may feel very different during its busiest month.

Before choosing a trending destination, it is sensible to do some homework. Look at the weather for the month you want to travel and find out whether local buses and trains are convenient. Some places become very busy during school holidays, festivals or special events. A beautiful island may be difficult to reach, and a famous restaurant may be fully booked.

You can also think about how visitors affect people who live there all year. Respecting local rules, avoiding unnecessary waste and supporting local services can make travel better for everyone. Online lists are useful for finding ideas, but they cannot tell you what will suit your family, health or budget. A less famous place may offer an amazing holiday, but it still needs careful planning.

Perhaps the best travel choice is not the place everybody is talking about. It is the place that matches your interests, budget and the way you enjoy spending time. A travel trend can help you discover somewhere new, but it should not make the decision for you.

=== Intermediate ===

Every year brings a new list of destinations that travellers are supposedly about to discover. Some are already famous, while others have spent years outside the international spotlight. What makes a location suddenly attractive, and can a travel company really predict where people will go next? A city may suddenly appear everywhere online after a popular film, a new flight route or a few beautiful photographs. The buzz can build long before most travellers know much about it. Expedia's Unpack '27 report, released in October 2026, identified destinations where searches for flights and accommodation had grown compared with the previous period. Its selections included Busan in South Korea, the Italian city of Perugia, Holbox in Mexico and Uruguay's Punta del Este. The list also featured places in Japan, Australia, Canada, Britain and the United States.

The destinations appeal to different kinds of travellers. Busan combines beaches with lively urban markets, while Perugia offers medieval streets and cultural festivals. Holbox is known for its quieter island atmosphere, and Punta del Este attracts holidaymakers to Atlantic beaches and seaside restaurants. Rather than following one theme, the list reflects growing curiosity about a wide variety of places.

The numbers deserve careful reading. Expedia compared search activity on its own platforms between September 2025 and July 2026 with a similar earlier period. Rising interest does not necessarily translate into completed bookings or future arrivals, and the results may differ from what other companies are seeing. There is no perfect destination for everybody. Someone looking for quiet walks may dislike the nightlife another visitor loves, so following a trend can leave people disappointed.

Increased attention also creates challenges. Tourism supports jobs and businesses, but sudden popularity can raise accommodation costs and place pressure on local infrastructure. Travellers may be attracted by images of quiet streets only to discover that other visitors have had the same idea.

The list also raises the question of what travellers should do when a destination receives sudden attention. Before booking, it is worth examining transport options, seasonal crowding, prices and whether visitors can access local attractions without placing unnecessary pressure on residents. Some destinations highlighted by Expedia may already have significant tourism, so the description 'hidden gem' can be misleading.

Local authorities may welcome additional visitors while worrying about housing costs and environmental damage. Travellers cannot solve those problems alone, but they can make informed choices about timing, accommodation and behaviour. A company's popularity ranking is only one piece of information, and its commercial interests are different from those of a local community deciding how much tourism it wants.

A useful travel list should therefore start a conversation rather than settle it. Where do you actually want to spend time, and what kind of experience are you hoping for? Choosing a destination because it fits your interests may be more rewarding than following the latest prediction about where everybody else is heading.

=== Advanced ===

Travel-industry forecasts are designed to create anticipation. A destination that barely featured in holiday conversations last year is suddenly presented as the place to visit next. But the relationship between online curiosity, actual bookings and a genuinely rewarding travel experience is far from straightforward. Predictions about the next fashionable destination have a way of becoming self-fulfilling. Media coverage generates curiosity, curiosity increases searches, and the resulting figures produce yet more headlines. Expedia's October 2026 Unpack '27 report used year-on-year growth in flight and accommodation searches to identify destinations attracting increasing interest. Among those highlighted were Busan in South Korea, Perugia in Italy, Holbox in Mexico and Punta del Este in Uruguay. The wider list also included Hyogo, the Tweed region, Chichester and several North American destinations.

The geographical range is part of the appeal. Busan combines metropolitan energy with beaches, Perugia offers a historic hilltop setting, and Punta del Este has long been associated with coastal holidays. Holbox promises a different pace on an island where vehicle access is limited. Such places are not necessarily undiscovered; many already have substantial tourism economies and established identities. That does not make the data worthless. It simply means that search growth is a measure of changing interest, not an impartial ranking of the world's most rewarding places.

The report's methodology is essential to understanding its claims. Expedia compared searches on its platforms from September 2025 to July 2026 against the corresponding earlier period. A percentage increase measures changes in interest within that system, not global visitor numbers or a guarantee of future popularity. A smaller destination may show a dramatic growth rate without attracting more tourists than a major capital.

There is also a paradox in promoting supposedly quieter alternatives. Publicity can redirect visitor pressure rather than reduce it. Rising demand may benefit restaurants and hotels but can also strain housing, transport and natural environments. Any discussion of emerging destinations should consider residents' priorities and the capacity of local infrastructure. Readers can use these lists as a starting point while checking seasonal conditions, local concerns and what they actually enjoy. Travel decisions deserve more than a fashionable postcode.

A responsible interpretation of a travel trend requires more than enthusiasm for percentage increases. Destinations differ in their capacity to accommodate new visitors, and promotional language can understate the consequences of rapidly rising demand. A coastal location may rely on a fragile ecosystem, while a historic city may face tension between tourist accommodation and residents' housing needs.

The fact that a company has recorded growing search interest does not establish that a place would benefit from substantial additional tourism. Travellers could therefore examine seasonal patterns, public transport, local accommodation policies and alternative periods for visiting before making plans. The most useful forecast encourages informed curiosity rather than a race to arrive before everybody else. Popularity alone is a poor substitute for understanding a place.

Perhaps the most useful lesson from a travel forecast is not to book immediately. It is to ask why these places are appealing, what sort of experience they offer and whether increased attention will change them. The best destination for 2027 will not be the same for everybody, regardless of what a ranking says.
