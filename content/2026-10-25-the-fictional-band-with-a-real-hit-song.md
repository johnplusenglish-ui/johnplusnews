title: The Fictional Band with a Real Hit Song
summary: HUNTR/X exists only in an animated film, yet its song became a real hit. A Netflix article shows how a team of real people built a fictional band's sound.

Can you be a fan of a band that does not exist? It sounds like a trick question, but it describes a real situation. In the animated film KPop Demon Hunters, released in June 2025, a pop group called HUNTR/X performs on stage and secretly protects the world from demons. Its biggest song is called "Golden".

The group is fictional, but its voices are not. According to a Netflix article published in February 2026, the singing voices of HUNTR/X belong to three real performers: EJAE, Audrey Nuna and Rei Ami. EJAE also sings the vocals of the character Rumi. Fictional does not mean computer-made. Behind every note there is a person.

The song was written by a team spread across the world. The directors, Maggie Kang and Chris Appelhans, and the producer, Michelle Wong, began with a seven-page memo explaining what the song had to do. It had to move the story forward and sound like a real hit. Separately, its main hook had to be hard to sing, because the character's voice was supposed to feel superhuman.

The team met every week online. They planned the song section by section before writing the lyrics, so that every part had a job in the story. A production team called THEBLACKLABEL created the beat, and the K-pop songwriter EJAE wrote the melody quickly, in a taxi on her way to a dentist's appointment. Together with the musical-theatre writer Mark Sonnenblick, she shaped it into lyrics. The music supervisor, Ian Eisendrath, says they wrote five or six versions before they found the final one.

One section in the middle changes the mood completely. The song drops from upbeat pop to something quieter and darker, so that the audience feels what the character feels at that moment. EJAE says she could draw on her own ten years as a K-pop trainee, which made the song feel personal.

Netflix's article also describes the song's success. It says that "Golden" has spent eight weeks at number one on the American Billboard Hot 100 chart and is approaching four billion streams. These figures come from Netflix, in February 2026, so it is sensible to treat them as the platform's claims, not independent measurements.

None of this requires you to like K-pop or to have seen the film. The real question is simple: what do fans connect with? A character, a voice, a story, or the people who made them? === Activity ===

Invent a fictional band. Give it a name, a first song title, a mood for the song and the job the song does in the story. Then name three imaginary people behind the scenes, for example a songwriter, a producer and a singer, and say what each one decides. Do not write lyrics from any existing song. Watching the film or listening to K-pop is optional, and nobody needs an account or a purchase to do this.
