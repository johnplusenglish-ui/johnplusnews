Imagine your favourite bag has a small hole. You have two choices. You can hide the hole with a stitch that nobody can see. Or you can cover it with a bright patch that everybody can see. Which choice feels more like you?

The listing said that teenagers would learn three skills: visible mending, patching and hand-stitching. Visible mending means that you don't hide a repair. You make it part of the design. People were asked to bring one small item that needed repair, no bigger than a hand. The goal was to give clothes a new life and to make less textile waste. Upcycling means turning something old into something better or more useful. Space in the workshop was limited.

A repair can be different things. You can mend a hole so the bag works again. Or you can give the bag a new look, with a patch in a surprising colour. Some people like repairs to be invisible. Other people like them to show, because the repair tells a story.

According to the listing, teenagers would learn essential textile repair skills, including visible mending, patching and hand-stitching. Two ideas are worth separating. Mending repairs damage so that something works again, and it can be invisible. Upcycling changes what an object is for, or makes it better than it was, so a torn T-shirt might become a bag. Visible mending sits between them. It fixes the damage, but it shows the fix as part of the design.

That is why a repair can say something. A neat invisible stitch says that nothing happened. A bright patch says that the object has a history and that someone chose how to deal with it. Some people find that more interesting than a new item, while others prefer clothes that look as if nothing has ever happened to them. Neither taste is wrong.

Remember what the source is. It's an event listing, so it tells us what was advertised, not how many people attended, what they thought or how much waste they saved. We shouldn't assume that repaired clothes are embarrassing or that expensive clothes are needed to take part.

So here is a question for your own wardrobe. If one item had a small tear, would you hide the repair, make it a feature or choose another solution? And what would you want that choice to say about you?
