title: A 99-Year-Old Above the Crowd
summary: Pauline Kana was passed over the heads of a Texas audience, and into the record books.

At a concert in Texas, people looked up and saw something extraordinary. High above their heads was a woman of ninety-nine. She was lying on a special stretcher while the crowd gently passed her towards the stage. People cheered, music played and she began waving her arms. Her name was Pauline Kana, and she was about to become the oldest person ever to crowd-surf.

The record took place in May 2026 at a concert by American singer Brantley Gilbert. Pauline was ninety-nine years and 274 days old. Her grandson, Ross Smith, had helped organise the adventure. The audience knew what was happening, and Pauline was safely supported as she moved above several rows of fans. When she reached the front, she stood up and enjoyed the moment on stage.

For Pauline, doing unusual things was nothing new. Years earlier, Ross had filmed his grandmother playing basketball with him. The funny video caught people's attention online. They carried on making videos together, and millions of people began following their adventures. Pauline tried activities that many younger people would think twice about. She enjoyed making others laugh and showing that growing older did not have to mean giving up on fun.

Ross came up with the idea of breaking a world record. Pauline was happy to give it a go, although crowd-surfing is not normally associated with people approaching their hundredth birthday. She wore a cheerful T-shirt and was carefully placed on the stretcher. At first she held on tightly. Then, as the crowd moved her along, she relaxed and started to enjoy herself.

She earned an official Guinness World Records title. In October 2026, now aged one hundred, Pauline described her experience in the Guardian. The story was not just about doing something funny at a concert. It was also about a close relationship between a grandmother and her grandson, who had found an unusual way to spend time together and share their sense of humour.

Of course, nobody should try a dangerous stunt simply to prove that age does not matter. Pauline's attempt was planned, supported and organised for a record. But her attitude offers something worth thinking about. People often stop trying new things because they believe they are too old. Pauline decided to turn that idea on its head. She did not wait for a perfect age to have an adventure. At ninety-nine, she went for it.

title: A World Record at a Rock Concert, Aged 99
summary: Pauline Kana was ninety-nine years and 274 days old. Her grandson planned the attempt, as he has planned their adventures for years.

The crowd at a country-rock concert in Texas had already come for an exciting evening. Then the singer announced an unusual guest: a ninety-nine-year-old grandmother who was about to attempt a world record. Moments later, Pauline Kana was lifted above the audience on a specially prepared stretcher. As dozens of hands passed her towards the stage, she loosened her grip and started waving. The cheering grew louder. By the time she reached the front, Pauline had achieved something no other person her age had officially done.

The record-breaking moment took place in May 2026 at a Brantley Gilbert concert in Bellville, Texas. According to Guinness World Records, Pauline was ninety-nine years and 274 days old. She became the oldest person to crowd-surf, travelling over several rows of spectators before reaching the stage. It was carefully arranged with the organisers and the audience, rather than an elderly concertgoer suddenly deciding to jump into a crowd. Nevertheless, the sight of her floating above the fans was unforgettable.

The person behind the adventure was her grandson, Ross Smith. Their unusual partnership began years earlier when Ross filmed Pauline blocking one of his basketball shots. The video caught on, and the pair went on to build an enormous social media following. Their clips celebrate Pauline's lively personality and their ability to make each other laugh. Instead of treating their age difference as a barrier, they have turned it into the heart of their relationship.

When Ross suggested trying to set a Guinness record, Pauline was keen to give it a go. At the concert, she initially held tightly to the sides of the stretcher. Once she realised how carefully the crowd was supporting her, she became more confident and began enjoying the ride. Afterwards, she appeared on stage with Ross. Guinness later confirmed the achievement, providing a permanent record of an afternoon that might otherwise have seemed impossible.

The story touches on an uncomfortable assumption about old age. We tend to imagine that people become less curious, less adventurous and less willing to take risks as the years pass. Yet physical limits do not automatically remove somebody's sense of humour or appetite for new experiences. Pauline's example does not mean everyone should take up extreme activities. It simply challenges the expectation that later life must always be quiet and predictable.

By October 2026, Pauline had turned one hundred and was still sharing stories about her adventures. The most appealing part of the record may be the relationship that made it possible. Ross did not merely encourage his grandmother from a distance; he joined in, planned the attempt and celebrated beside her. What began with a silly basketball video developed into years of shared memories. Pauline's journey above a concert crowd was spectacular, but the connection between two generations was perhaps the real achievement.

title: Who Says a Rock Concert Has an Age Limit?
summary: A carefully arranged record attempt is not proof that age imposes no limits. It is a reminder that those limits need not define somebody's ambitions.

Concertgoers at a country-rock show in Texas were prepared for noise, excitement and perhaps a few surprises. They were unlikely, however, to expect a ninety-nine-year-old woman to be carried above their heads. Yet that was precisely the spectacle unfolding at a Brantley Gilbert performance in Bellville in May 2026. Pauline Kana lay on a specially prepared stretcher as the audience passed her carefully towards the stage. Initially gripping the edges, she soon began waving to the cheering crowd, visibly delighted by her extraordinary journey.

Guinness World Records subsequently recognised Pauline as the oldest person to crowd-surf, recording her age as ninety-nine years and 274 days. The attempt was coordinated rather than spontaneous, with the musician introducing the occasion and spectators supporting the stretcher. That distinction matters: it was a planned public event, not an invitation for older people to attempt unsafe stunts. Nonetheless, the sight of Pauline waving from a stretcher above the crowd was not the kind of image a ninety-nine-year-old usually appears in.

The adventure grew out of a remarkable creative partnership with her grandson, Ross Smith. Years earlier, Ross had filmed Pauline playing basketball with him, capturing a moment when she successfully blocked his shot. The clip caught on, and their humorous videos gradually attracted millions of followers. Rather than portraying Pauline as a passive grandmother watching younger people have adventures, the pair built their content around her mischievous personality and willingness to join in. Their age difference became a source of comedy, not a reason to keep them apart.

When Ross came up with the idea of a Guinness title, Pauline was apparently delighted to give it a try. On the day, she wore a T-shirt celebrating the pleasures of growing older and took her place on the stretcher. The crowd began chanting as she travelled over their heads; what could easily have become a nerve-racking experience turned into something resembling a triumphant parade. At the front, she joined Ross on stage and celebrated. Later, a recording showed her proudly displaying the certificate confirming her place in the record books.

Behind the entertaining spectacle sits a more serious question about the way society talks about ageing. Later life is frequently described in terms of decline, dependence and things that can no longer be done. Those realities matter, but they do not account for the whole person. Curiosity, humour and the desire to experience something new do not vanish on a particular birthday. Pauline's story is not proof that age imposes no limits, but a reminder that those limits need not decide somebody's ambitions or enjoyment of life.

By the time Pauline reflected on the adventure in October 2026, she had celebrated her hundredth birthday. Yet the real story reaches beyond the record itself. Ross and Pauline have spent years making each other laugh, taking on unfamiliar challenges and creating an archive of shared experiences. Their partnership turns a familiar family relationship into an ongoing collaboration, one in which both generations seem to benefit. The lasting image is not simply a grandmother being lifted through a crowd. It is a woman refusing to let other people's expectations decide when the fun should stop.
